Samplers ​

Equipped with the domains defined in Domains, we now present the machinery of how the next candidate is selected in BigHOPs.jl. Selection of the next candidate is handled by a sampler , which, depending on the sampler, may depend on the previous candidates or not.

One way the samplers in BigHOPs.jl can be divided is by whether they evaluate the objective f at the full resource or not:

  • Full-resource samplers evaluate f once, at the full resource (e.g. all training epochs), with the number of evaluations specified upfront.

  • Successive-halving samplers start (potentially many) trials at a small resource and promote only some fraction of the best trials at that resource level to the next stage, where they are evaluated with more resource.

Another way to divide the samplers is by whether the sampler is adaptive, i.e. whether previous candidates inform the candidates sampled in the future. Therefore, we group the samplers into a   matrix by these two features:

Non-adaptiveAdaptive
Full-resourceRandomSampler, LHSampler, SobolSamplerGPSampler
Successive-halvingHyperband, ASHADEHB, BOHB

For reproducibility (in so far as it is possible under things like schedulers and differing resource contention at different reruns) each sampler has a default seed, which can be changed.

Full-resource samplers ​

The number of objective evaluations for each full-resource sampler is specified by setting n when creating a Hyperoptimizer.

Full-resource non-adaptive samplers ​

RandomSampler ​

BigHOPs.RandomSampler Type
julia
RandomSampler(rng=StableRNG(1))

Draw each trial's hyperparameters independently at random. Seeded by default, so repeated searches propose the same trials.

source

The simplest baseline, and also the default inner sampler of Hyperband and ASHA.

LHSampler ​

BigHOPs.LHSampler Type
julia
LHSampler(; rng=StableRNG(1))

Draw all n trials up front as a Latin hypercube, which spreads them evenly along every hyperparameter. Warns if n is too small to cover every combination of discrete values. Each LHSampler() can back only one Hyperoptimizer.

source

Uses the QuasiMonteCarlo.jl implementation.

SobolSampler ​

BigHOPs.SobolSampler Type
julia
SobolSampler(; rng=StableRNG(1))

Draw each trial's hyperparameters as the next point of a Sobol sequence, moved by one random offset drawn from rng and wrapped around at 1, so successive trials spread over the space more evenly than independent random draws. Like RandomSampler, it generates points as they are asked for and needs no fixed number of trials; after any power-of-2 number of trials, each hyperparameter's range is covered evenly.

source

Uses the Sobol.jl implementation.

Full-resource adaptive samplers ​

GPSampler ​

BigHOPs.GPSampler Type
julia
GPSampler(gp; next_point, noise, n_initial, initial=SobolSampler(),
          fit_hyperparams=nothing, standardize=BigHOPs.zscore,
          strict_initialization=true, rng=StableRNG(1))

Model the objective as a Gaussian process over the unit-encoded hyperparameters and let next_point choose each new trial once the initial trials are done. From then on, the GP is conditioned on every Completed trial after each tell; during the initial trials it is not. Nominal hyperparameters enter the kernel through their ordered unit encoding.

  • gp: an AbstractGPs prior, e.g. GP(Matern52Kernel()).

  • next_point(posterior, d, rng): return the next trial as a point in [0, 1]^d, e.g. ThompsonSampling.

  • noise: the observation noise variance, or nothing when fit_hyperparams returns one. A noise returned by fit_hyperparams replaces it, with a warning the first time if it was a number; the one in use is ho.sampler.noise[].

  • n_initial: how many trials initial draws before next_point is used. No further trial is proposed until all of them have finished; next_point then takes over from those that completed, with a warning if that is fewer than n_initial.

  • initial: the sampler that draws the initial trials, SobolSampler by default, or RandomSampler, or LHSampler when strict_initialization is true.

  • fit_hyperparams(X, y): called before each conditioning; return either the GP to condition on, e.g. one with refitted kernel hyperparameters, or a (gp, noise) pair to refit the noise variance too. When nothing, gp is used as given.

  • standardize(y): transform the observed values before fit_hyperparams and conditioning; the default shifts and scales them to mean 0 and standard deviation 1. When nothing, they are used as given.

  • strict_initialization: when false, each initial trial that does not complete gets an extra trial from initial instead, with a warning, until n_initial have completed; the unsuccessful ones stay in the results, and new proposals wait only while the initial trials still running could complete the set.

next_point, fit_hyperparams and standardize are saved in checkpoints by name, so pass named functions or callable structs such as ThompsonSampling.

source

ThompsonSampling(n_candidates; jitter) (Kandasamy et al., 2018) is the provided next_point: it draws the posterior at n_candidates Sobol points and returns the one where the draw is lowest.

julia
using AbstractGPs

sampler = GPSampler(GP(Matern52Kernel()); next_point=ThompsonSampling(512; jitter=1e-6),
                    noise=1e-6, n_initial=10)
ho = Hyperoptimizer(f, domains; sampler, n=50)

Successive-halving samplers ​

Successive halving as a hyperparameter optimization strategy was first introduced in Jamieson and Talwalkar (2016), as a single bracket of rungs. Li et al. (2018) extended it to hedge your bets given some budget, setting up a series of brackets split into rungs, where each successive rung evaluates the objective f with a progressively larger resource. With the total budget being approximately equal between brackets, the first bracket is the most aggressive, evaluating the objective many times with just enough resource, such that the good candidates would be distinguishable from the bad ones. The best fraction of the candidates is then promoted to the next rung and trained with a larger resource. The second bracket starts off with a higher minimal resource, following the same procedure, until the last bracket, where a few objective evaluations are made at the full resource.

The computation given to a single evaluation of f is formalized via the notion of resource – training epochs, amount of data, etc., whatever the problem context dictates. Other parameters used to set up the schedule for the brackets and the promotions are:

  • – the largest resource level, given to the trials that survive every round;

  • – the smallest resource level, where the most aggressive bracket starts;

  • – the factor between rounds, after each round, the best of the trials continue with times the resource;

  • – repeat the whole schedule that many times.

Note that when a trial is promoted, it does not have to start from scratch. Using the Stateful wrapper over your objective allows Hyperband, ASHA and BOHB to continue training from where the trial finished in the previous rung, potentially saving a significant part of the budget, see Stateful objectives.

r is reserved

Successive-halving samplers pass the resource level to the objective as x.r, in the same NamedTuple as the hyperparameters, so r cannot be the name of a hyperparameter.

Finally, contrary to the full-resource samplers, the number of objective evaluations of f is set by sampler's parameters, rather than n.

Successive-halving non-adaptive samplers ​

Hyperband ​

BigHOPs.Hyperband Type
julia
Hyperband(; R, η=3, r_min=1, iterations=1, inner=RandomSampler())

Successive halving over brackets that start at resource levels from r_min up to R: after each rung the best 1/η of trials continue with η times the resource, and a rung must finish before any of its trials are promoted (Li et al., 2018, https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1603.06560). iterations repeats the whole schedule, inner draws the starting configurations, and the objective receives the resource level as x.r.

When every open bracket is waiting on results and the executor has spare capacity, the next bracket starts, from the next iteration if needed; earlier brackets keep priority for every new trial.

source

ASHA ​

BigHOPs.ASHA Type
julia
ASHA(; R, η=3, r_min=1, iterations=1, inner=RandomSampler())

Asynchronous Hyperband, with the same schedule and arguments: a trial is promoted as soon as it is among the best 1/η completed so far in its rung, instead of waiting for the rung to finish (Li et al., 2020, https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1810.05934). This keeps workers busy under Threaded or DistributedQueue.

source

Successive-halving adaptive samplers ​

DEHB ​

BigHOPs.DEHB Type
julia
DEHB(; R, η=3, r_min=1, iterations=1, F=0.5, crossover=0.5, rng=StableRNG(1))

Hyperband's schedule with differential evolution in place of random draws, following the paper (Awad et al., 2021, https://doi.org/10.24963/ijcai.2021/296), with the details it leaves open taken from the reference implementation (https://github.com/automl/DEHB).

The first rung of the first bracket is drawn uniformly at random, and every other first-rung trial is evolved from its own level's subpopulation, with a random target standing in for a slot that is still empty. In the first iteration, later rungs promote the best configurations from the level below unchanged; in later iterations every trial is evolved from the level below. Every trial trains from scratch: a Stateful objective never receives a pre_artefact and only has its post_artefact recorded.

source

BOHB ​

BigHOPs.BOHB Type
julia
BOHB(; R, η=3, r_min=1, iterations=1, ρ=1/3, q=0.15, N_s=64, N_min=nothing, b_w=3,
     min_bandwidth=1e-3, rng=StableRNG(1))

Hyperband's schedule with the first rung of each bracket drawn from kernel density estimates, following the paper (Falkner et al., 2018, https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.01774), with the details it leaves open taken from the reference implementation (https://github.com/automl/HpBandSter). Promotions are as in Hyperband.

A fraction ρ of draws, and every draw before a resource level has enough completed trials, is uniform at random. Otherwise the N completed trials at the largest such level are split into the best max(N_min, ⌊qN⌋) and the rest, and the draw is whichever of N_s samples from the good density, with bandwidths widened by b_w except on Nominal, has the highest ratio of good to bad density. N_min defaults to the number of hyperparameters plus one, and anything lower throws an ArgumentError.

source

The model needs more completed trials as the number of hyperparameters grows. By default the KDE machinery starts at about   completed trials at a specific resource level. Both the good and the bad set need more than trials since a KDE fitted on fewer points in dimensions is not very informative.
